# 1. Project Setup

- Format: 16:9 landscape master illustrations for an interactive digital storybook / educational experience.

- Audience: Ages 6–10.

- Structure: Episode 1 — The Great Churning and the Blue-Throated Lord (target 7:30); Episode 2 — The Treasures of the Ocean and Lord Dhanvantari (target 7:30).

- Art direction: Madhubani-inspired 2D Indian folk-art illustration. Treat this as an original inspired adaptation, not a claim that every design detail represents one uniform traditional practice.

- Primary output: Production-ready visual descriptions and AI image-generation prompts, with interaction states and continuity notes.

- Implementation: Interactive app or experience supporting touchscreen input; support camera-based pinch tracking for the ocean-waking interaction where available, with a tap alternative.

- Core message: Health, compassion, wisdom, love and kindness are among life's greatest treasures. Wealth can bring comfort, but the way we care for ourselves and others matters deeply.

## Important production rules

- Generate illustration artwork without text, captions, buttons, counters, logos or watermarks. Add these separately in the target application.

- Keep all master scenes in 16:9 landscape. Do not crop essential faces, hands, the mountain, serpent, amrit pot or interaction targets to make room for interface elements.

- Keep the two-choice interactions for Kurma and Shiva non-punitive. Both choices must converge on the same canonical story outcome; only the immediate animation changes.

- The source script specifies four lotus flowers for the interactive treasure discovery, with Kamadhenu, Airavata, Kalpavriksha and Lakshmi as the on-screen discoveries. The broader narrated treasure sequence also mentions a celestial horse and jewels. Preserve that distinction in staging.

- The supplied Dhanvantari shloka is the script version to place in the experience. Have a qualified Sanskrit reviewer verify spelling, sandhi, pronunciation, transliteration and meaning before release.

# 2. Global Visual Direction

## Madhubani-inspired 2D art language

- Use flat, saturated colour areas with confident dark outlines, occasional double outlines, and dense decorative linework inside characters, clothing, mountains, waves, flowers and props.

- Build forms from clear silhouettes, almond-shaped expressive eyes, stylised hands, traditional garments and ornamental jewellery.

- Use repeating fish, lotus petals, scalloped wave bands, sun and star motifs, floral geometry, parallel lines and symmetrical borders as a consistent visual vocabulary.

- Create depth through layered placement, scale and overlapping patterned shapes—not through realistic perspective, 3D volume, gradients, cinematic lens effects or photorealistic lighting.

- Make the visual action immediately readable for children. Intricate patterns should enrich, not obscure, faces, hands, the serpent rope, Kurma beneath the mountain, Shiva's blue throat, the treasure objects or Dhanvantari's amrit pot.

## Palette progression

- Episode 1 / Scene 1 — Ocean of Secrets: deep indigo, midnight blue, turquoise, cream and tiny golden lights; calm, curious atmosphere.

- Episode 1 / Scene 2 — Mountain sinking and Kurma: turquoise and indigo water, warm gold on the mountain, Vasuki's red/teal/cream pattern, then a reassuring blue-white glow beneath the waves.

- Episode 1 / Scene 3 — Great Churning: increasingly energetic teal, turquoise, indigo, cream foam and golden currents; dark plum begins to appear at the end.

- Episode 1 / Scene 4 — Shiva and Halahala: plum, violet and smoky grey for the threat; clear Shiva blue, cream and gold for protection and resolution.

- Episode 2 / Scene 1 — Treasures: luminous turquoise, coral, cream, lotus pink and gold; each treasure has a distinct colour silhouette.

- Episode 2 / Scene 2 — Dhanvantari: calm turquoise ocean, gold-white radiance, yellow-gold garments and a clearly readable gold amrit vessel.

- Episode 2 / Scene 3 — Shloka: warm cream, antique gold, restrained turquoise and coral; peaceful and uncluttered.

- Episode 2 / Scene 4 — True Wealth: bright gold vessel with five distinct colour-coded symbols: health, love/family, kindness, wisdom, gold/jewels.

- Episode 2 / Scene 5 — Family celebration: saffron, vermilion, marigold orange, leaf green, cream and warm gold; end with a single diya against deep blue.

## Composition and accessibility rules

- Keep the main focal action in the central 65–70% of the frame and large enough for tablet viewing.

- Preserve a calm text-safe area wherever narration, choices, shloka or activity controls will appear. Do not generate interface text inside the illustration.

- Interactive targets must have clear silhouettes and visible active/inactive states. Use shape, position and motion as well as colour to communicate state.

- Avoid frightening poison imagery, graphic injury, sick or suffering bodies, monstrous asuras, or threatening facial exaggeration. Halahala is symbolic supernatural vapour.

- Use the same character references, garment colours, facial features, jewellery, attributes and proportions throughout.

- Suggested animation is restrained and folk-art compatible: simple layer slides, opening lotus petals, ripples, patterned glow rings, small repeated movements and clean match cuts.

# 3. Character and Reusable Asset Guide

## Main characters

### Lord Vishnu

Serene blue-complexioned deity with traditional golden crown, yellow garments and gold jewellery. In the established reference design, show four clearly separated arms carrying shankha (conch), chakra (discus), gada (mace) and lotus. His expression and gestures are calm and reassuring. Use this form for the guidance scene; design Kurma as a separate, clearly identifiable tortoise form.

### Kurma — Vishnu's tortoise form

Large, dignified stylised tortoise beneath the water. Broad patterned shell must visibly support the underside of Mount Mandara. Show the head and at least some flippers so children can read the action. Keep the design friendly and powerful, not comic or animal-realistic.

### Mount Mandara

Tall central mountain with a stable, recognisable silhouette. Decorate its slopes with repeated leaves, flowers, diamonds, fine parallel lines and geometric bands. Use the same silhouette and pattern layout in all churning scenes. In the sinking state, move the mountain vertically downward without changing its design.

### Vasuki

Long serpent king used as the churning rope. His body has a continuous pattern in red, teal, cream and gold, with a clear head and hood. Keep his body coil readable around the mountain and leave a visible rope segment extending to each group. Expressive but non-threatening.

### Devas and asuras

Use small recurring groups with consistent faces, crowns and costumes. Devas use cream, blue, green and gold; asuras use rust, maroon, green and dark gold. Both groups should appear dignified, expressive and capable of cooperation. During the churning, show their hands gripping the two ends of Vasuki and their bodies leaning in opposite directions.

### Lord Shiva / Neelkanth

Calm, compassionate blue-toned complexion; matted hair with crescent moon and Ganga emerging from it; rudraksha beads, traditional ornaments, tiger-skin garment and trident nearby. His blue throat must be a distinct, contained luminous focal point. Do not show poison dripping, wounds or bodily harm.

### Goddess Lakshmi

Graceful, serene goddess seated on a large pink lotus, wearing a red or coral sari, gold jewellery and a crown. Use a symmetrical lotus and gentle golden halo. Keep the silhouette distinct from the patterned ocean.

### Kamadhenu

A benevolent divine cow, elegant and calm, with a clear cow silhouette, ornamental forehead and body patterns, and a gentle expression. Avoid overloading the figure with too many tiny symbolic details.

### Celestial horse

A radiant white celestial horse with a flowing patterned mane and a clear four-legged silhouette. It appears in the narrated treasure sequence; do not substitute it for Kamadhenu in the four interactive lotus discoveries unless the source interaction is intentionally revised.

### Airavata

Majestic white celestial elephant with clearly readable trunk, ears and legs, decorated with subtle traditional motifs and a restrained ornamental headpiece.

### Kalpavriksha

Magnificent wish-fulfilling tree with a symmetrical, ornamental canopy, patterned branches, flowers, fruits and small birds. Keep the trunk and crown readable as a single tree silhouette.

### Lord Dhanvantari

Radiant, calm blue-complexioned divine figure with traditional golden crown, yellow-gold garments and jewellery. The gold pot of amrit is the hero prop and must be prominently visible. Follow one reviewed iconographic reference for arm count and attributes; do not randomly mix attributes between images.

### Children in the shloka scene

A small, diverse group of Indian children in simple festive or traditional clothing, seen in the foreground with hands joined in namaste. Keep their expressions calm and sincere. They should not obscure Dhanvantari or the shloka-safe area.

### Dhanteras family

A warm multigenerational Indian family preparing diyas together. Reuse the same adult, grandparent and two children across the closing scene. Keep their clothing colours and patterns consistent. The family should feel welcoming and collaborative rather than posed like a formal portrait.

Character acting: Devas look concerned but hopeful; Vishnu's posture is open and steady. The scene should communicate a wise plan, not a crisis.

Palette: Midnight blue, indigo, turquoise, cream and tiny antique-gold accents.

### Interactive moment — Wake up the ocean

Visual state: Begin with mostly still water and several small, softly glowing lights beneath the surface.

- Instruction shown separately in UI: “Tap the glowing lights to awaken the ocean!”

- Touchscreen: child taps each light. Camera mode: child pinches fingers together over a light to activate it. Always offer a tap alternative if camera access is unavailable.

- Each activation produces a circular golden ripple, a soft chime and a brighter light. After several activations, waves begin to move, lotus flowers open and the whole ocean glows.

- Completion state: The ocean is visibly awake, with several golden ripple rings and a brighter central glow. A rising magical sound signals completion.

Animation note: Use small ripple loops and simple layered movement. Avoid a dramatic camera move; the visual change should come from the water and lights.

Transition: The central golden ripple widens and becomes the circular base shape beneath Mount Mandara in Scene 2.
